# Licence history

Being straight about this, because software licence changes deserve it.

## 2026-07-29 — MIT

TokenBrake was published under the MIT licence. **Everything released under MIT stays under
MIT, permanently.** That grant cannot be, and is not being, withdrawn. Anyone who obtained a
copy under MIT keeps every right MIT gave them, forever, including commercial use.

## 2026-08-13 — PolyForm Small Business 1.0.0

New work is released under the PolyForm Small Business License 1.0.0.

**What that actually changes for you:**

- **Individuals: nothing.** Free, including using it at your job.
- **Companies under 100 people and under $1M revenue: nothing.** Free.
- **Companies above both of those:** a commercial licence — one payment, one company,
  unlimited machines and agents, no renewal.

The source stays public and readable. You can still read every line, audit it, fork it, and
change it. This isn't a source-closing.

## Why

Honestly: TokenBrake went MIT because collecting money looked harder than it was, not because
giving it away was the plan. Once that turned out to be solvable, the terms should reflect what
was actually intended — free for the people it was built for, paid by the companies it saves
real money.

This change was made at two weeks old, with no user base to disrupt. That was deliberate. If
you're going to change terms, the honest time to do it is before anyone has built on them, not
after.

If you adopted TokenBrake under MIT in those two weeks and this creates a problem for you,
get in touch. That's a conversation worth having, not a fight.
